# Offline MusicBrainz Layer Study and Artist Vocabulary Proposal

Observed September 18, 2026; MusicBrainz snapshot `20260912-002318`. This study
extended the [initial coverage study](01.md) with five contemporary-biased profiles
and one historical profile with more MPB, samba, and jazz. Profiles, artists,
titles, and identifiers are anonymized. It tested whether context could improve
results without being mistaken for evidence about a listened recording.

## Method

Each profile contributed 50 Last.fm top tracks. Contemporary profiles used
`period=overall`; the historical profile used `period=6month` (859 plays), because
its overall ranking did not represent the intended recent listening variety.
The study used an offline MusicBrainz full dump, avoiding public API `503` and
shared-IP effects but retaining catalog gaps. Identity resolution distinguished
track from recording MBIDs, used conservative text matching, and left ambiguous
identities unresolved. Instrument names were mapped to the project taxonomy;
track and play reach were measured separately, with a control excluding `voice`.

Three layers were measured: direct recording/track instrument credits; credits
on a matching release that cannot be assigned to the listened track; and an
artist-catalog instrument appearing on at least three distinct recordings.
Only the first layer qualifies as direct track evidence. Release matching used
`medium.release` (the dump columns are `id`, `release`, `position`); confusing
`position` with the release ID silently loses matches.

## Results

The historical sample resolved 41 of 50 tracks and found release context for
32: 23 via exact track MBIDs and nine via conservative album matching.

| Historical layer            | Tracks reached |      Plays reached | Artists |
| --------------------------- | -------------: | -----------------: | ------: |
| Direct track evidence       |   5 / 50 (10%) | 183 / 859 (21.30%) |       5 |
| Direct plus release context |   7 / 50 (14%) | 206 / 859 (23.98%) |       6 |
| Recurrent artist catalog    |  40 / 50 (80%) | 753 / 859 (87.66%) |      26 |
| Any mapped layer            |  41 / 50 (82%) | 763 / 859 (88.82%) |      27 |

Removing `voice` did not change its direct coverage. Direct credits included
double bass, drums, guitar, piano, and horns; release context added narrow
detail such as cuica and flute. The artist catalog yielded a much broader
vocabulary, which cannot be assigned to every listened track.

These are six case studies, not a population estimate. The original 300-track
pooled scan used the historical profile's earlier **overall** sample, so its
figures are not the sum of the table: direct evidence reached 57 tracks (19.0%)
and 14,991 plays (19.56%); requiring a non-vocal instrument reduced this to 41
tracks (13.67%) and 11,864 plays (15.48%). Release context appeared on two
tracks and added one to the direct-plus-release union. Recurrent artist evidence
reached 199 tracks (66.33%) and 50,248 plays (65.56%).

## Interpretation and decision

Direct evidence was sparse even for resolved recordings. Release context added
little reach and must retain its release scope. Artist recurrence gave useful
context for some profiles but varied from 20% to 94% track reach across the
contemporary samples. It answers which instruments recur in documented work by
listened artists, not which instruments occur in the tracks played. A raw union
is also misleading: the historical sample had more than 40 mapped instrument
names after the three-recording requirement, partly from long careers and
detailed sessions.

The study therefore supported a separately gated **Artist Vocabulary** by
family, with distinct-recording counts, prevalence, artist balancing, and
provenance. Its `track_reach` and `play_reach` must never increase direct
`coverage_tracks` or `coverage_plays`, or unlock track-dependent discovery,
sound balance, or temperament. A candidate score combines capped artist
listening weight, documented family prevalence, and evidence quality. Three
recordings per artist was a research starting point, not a validated universal
threshold. Eligibility also needs artist diversity and a concentration limit.

Results should identify their MusicBrainz snapshot and methodology version,
qualifying artists and recordings, reach, unresolved identities, and the fact
that missing credits do not prove absence. Thresholds require calibration on a
larger cohort, including electronic, rap, orchestral, traditional, compilation,
collaboration, and large-discography cases. The offline index also needs a
reusable architecture benchmark for build time, size, R2 and CI cost, and query
latency. The current [methodology](../methodology.md) and [API contract](../api-contract.md)
document the subsequently adopted product rules.
