# Instrumental Coverage Study

Observed September 12, 2026. This anonymized study measured how often the first
50 `period=overall` Last.fm tracks in each of five profiles had usable MusicBrainz
instrument credits. Profiles, artists, titles, and recording identifiers are omitted.

## Method

Identity resolution tried a Last.fm MBID as a recording, resolved track MBIDs to
recordings, then used conservative artist/title matching. Only recording- or
track-scoped instrument relationships counted as coverage. A resolved recording
without such a credit remained uncovered; artist, genre, composition, and
release-level context did not count. Results reflect the data available on the
observation date.

## Results

| Measure                                |                  Result |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------: |
| Tracks with a Last.fm MBID             |       141 / 250 (56.4%) |
| Recordings resolved                    |       156 / 250 (62.4%) |
| Tracks with direct instrument evidence |        26 / 250 (10.4%) |
| Plays on covered tracks                | 6,388 / 56,165 (11.37%) |
| Raw instrument relationships           |                      83 |

Resolution paths were 79 recording MBIDs, 48 track MBIDs, 27 exact text matches,
and two normalized text matches; 94 tracks remained unresolved. The 48 track
MBIDs show why a Last.fm MBID cannot automatically be treated as a recording ID.

These pooled totals are not an average or a population estimate: histories
overlap, play counts differ, and four profiles favored contemporary rap, trap,
hip-hop, and internet scenes. The fifth had more indie/pop-rock, pop, and K-pop.
Of the 156 resolved recordings, 138 (88.5%) were released after 2010 and only
two before 2000. Well-cataloged releases and conventional personnel credits were
easier to resolve and document. MusicBrainz also returned transient `503` errors;
60 retries and a targeted recheck found no further accepted credits, but an
unavailable response was never treated as proof of missing credits.

## Implications

Identity resolution and instrument evidence are separate bottlenecks. Track and
play coverage must be considered together, with minimum track and artist counts.
Generic or release-only credits cannot establish an instrument on a recording.
Sparse evidence calls for visible `partial` or `insufficient` results, independently
gated report sections, and versioned methodology changes. These safeguards are
described in the current [analysis methodology](../methodology.md).
